Letter names, vowel marks and shortcuts

Any listed name spelling is accepted. Shortcuts are the short codes after each name; edit them to suit yourself (separate several with commas). Names like vet, khaf and fe count for their letter. Shin and Sin are separate cards, and their dot is always shown, even with vowel points off. For words, type the letters in reading order: the rightmost Hebrew letter first. Separate letters with spaces, or run shortcuts together if you like (bayz is fine only when it can be read one way).

Mode 3: reading with vowels

Type each letter by its sound, then the vowel mark written on it, working right to left: מֹשֶׁה is m oh sh e. Letter names and the editable shortcuts above aren’t used here. Letters that sound alike share a code (Tet and Tav are both t), and a dagesh dot decides b/v, k/kh and p/f. A dot inside a vav (וּ) is the vowel shureq; a dagesh is otherwise not typed. Helper letters that aren’t sounded are left out, though their vowel is still typed: a vav carrying holam or shureq (רוּת is r u t), a yod after hiriq, tsere or segol (בֵּית is b ee t), and a final he with no vowel (יְהוּדָה ends d aa). A sheva on the last letter is silent too and isn’t typed (אֱלִימֶלֶךְ ends l e kh), and so is a final aleph with no vowel (הוּא is h u). Inside a word, an aleph with no vowel after a vowel is usually silent, so ' there is optional (וַתֹּאמֶר is v a t oh m e r or v a t oh ' m e r).
